[#xactor-lifecycle]
== Lifecycle

Every actor has a status, which `status_of` returns. It starts `active`, the
one status in which it receives messages, and it ends in one of the others,
each final:

[cols="1,1,3,2",options="header"]
|===
| Status | Value | How an actor gets it | What it keeps

| `active`
| 1
| Every actor starts here: `create_actor`, `spawn` and a turn's `spawn_child`
  create it active.
| Its mailbox and its timers.

| `done`
| 2
| Its turn called `finish(output)`.
| The output, for `output_of`.

| `error`
| 3
| Its `handle` returned an error.
| The error, for `error_of`.

| `stopped`
| 4
| The host's `stop`, its parent's `stop_child`, or either of them for one of
  its ancestors, stopped it; or its own turn called `stop()`.
| Nothing.
|===

The values are fixed and never reused, because a system that persists its
actors rebuilds them from these numbers.

Actors form families. An actor spawned in a turn is a child of that turn's
actor: it records the actor as its parent, which `turn.parent()` returns, and
the parent records its children in the order it spawned them. An actor the
host created has no parent. A family is stopped together, from its parent or
by the host, each actor after its own descendants, siblings in the order they
were spawned.

The supervisor is actor 1, and its children took the next addresses in the
order it spawned them: the doubler 2, the failing child 3 and the idle one 4.
The doubler finished with 42 and is `done`; the failing child returned
`invalid_argument` and ended with the status `error`, while the rest of the
system went on; the idle child and the supervisor are still `active`. Neither
of the children that ended told the supervisor: xactor does not tell a parent
that its child has ended. A child that must tell its parent sends it a
message, to `turn.parent()`, in the turn it ends.

`stop` visits the family in its order, the children 2, 3 and 4 and then their
parent 1, and stops each that is still active: the doubler and the failing
child stay as they ended, so it stops 4 and then 1, and returns them in that
order. The `start` delivered afterwards is accepted, since the address names
an actor, and dropped, since the actor has ended: nothing is delivered.

The rules of the lifecycle:

* *Only an active actor receives messages.* Once an actor has ended, its
  pending messages are dropped, never delivered and never logged, and so is
  every later message to it. Dropping is not an error of the sender's, which
  cannot know the actor has ended.
* *An actor finishes once.* `finish(output)` makes it `done` and keeps the
  output; a second `finish` is `invalid_argument` and changes nothing.
  `output_of` is `nullptr` unless the actor is `done`, and `error_of` is empty
  unless it is `error`.
* *An actor ends in one way.* An error that `handle` returns in the turn that
  finished or stopped its actor changes nothing: the actor stays as it ended.
* *Stopping a child stops its family.* `turn.stop_child(child)` stops `child`
  and every actor descended from it that is still active, in the family's
  order, and returns them in that order; their mailboxes and timers are
  dropped. A member that had already ended stays as it ended, and its
  descendants are stopped all the same. It is `invalid_argument` for anything
  but a child of the caller: the caller itself, a grandchild, an address that
  names no actor. The host's `stop(actor)` does the same for any actor.
* *Ending alone.* `turn.stop()` ends the actor handling the message as
  `stopped`, alone. An actor that finishes, fails or stops itself leaves its
  children running: they are its logic's to stop, with `stop_child`, before it
  ends.
* *An ended actor creates nothing.* In the rest of the turn that ended it, an
  actor spawns no child and arms no timer: `spawn_child` and `wake_at` are
  `invalid_argument`, and `spawn_child` constructs nothing. It can still send.
